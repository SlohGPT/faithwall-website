## 1. Positioning & Moat

FaithWall is the only iOS app purpose-built to put a daily Bible verse on the iPhone lock screen, and the site's search moat is exactly that intersection. faithwall.app holds the leading editorial position across the "bible verse lock screen app" query family, including first-place rankings on many long-tail permutations.

Why we win, structurally:

- **Perfect query-product fit.** For any permutation of {bible verse, scripture} × {lock screen, wallpaper, widget} × {iphone, app}, FaithWall is the literal answer, not an adjacent one.
- **Only differentiated editorial in the SERP.** These results pages are App Store listings plus content-farm listicles. A real indie developer writing honest, tested, screenshot-backed pages wins on trust.
- **Freshness advantage.** Incumbent editorial on the big "best Bible app" queries predates iOS 16 lock screens entirely. Freshness also drives AI citations directly: roughly four-fifths of lists cited by ChatGPT were updated within the past year, a quarter within two months (Allsopp/Ahrefs, Dec 2025).
- **AI answer engines already cite us.** Quotable, self-contained claims near the top of every page are an asset class. But two 2026 findings bound this play: AI systems decouple *citation* from *recommendation* — self-promotional roundups get cited while the authoring brand is excluded from the actual recommendation most of the time (Ray, Jun 2026) — and even large brands report AI citations producing under 1–2% of traffic (detailed.com tracker). So the AI surface is a **brand-visibility channel, not a near-term traffic channel**, and the recommendation itself is won by third-party validation, not by our own pages. That is what §8 existed for (RETIRED 2026-11-16, v3: authority is accepted as fixed).

Structural ceilings to respect:

1. App Store product listings permanently occupy several top slots on the money SERPs. Target everywhere: **top editorial result** (effectively top-3 visible).
2. The overwhelming majority of our impressions carry no identifiable query (AI surfaces + anonymized long-tail). Pages are judged per §5 on the demand type they actually face: legible transactional pages on clicks, anonymized surfaces on quotability and topical support.
3. **The position-5-to-3 gap is an authority gap, not a content gap.** The March 2026 core update rewarded specialist sites with demonstrated authority and punished aggregator/comparison patterns; a domain-level authority score consistent with the leaked `siteAuthority` field plausibly caps how far strong pages rise on a weak domain. More on-page polish does not cross this gap. Mentions, citations, reviews, and branded demand do. **v3, 2026-11-16: with outreach ruled out, this gap is accepted as fixed. No action is planned for it.**

Strategy in one sentence: **hold the lock-screen moat, win the "best Bible app" hub with depth plus authority, and build the brand/off-site layer that v1 was missing — because brand is now the input that everything else (rankings, AI recommendations, clicks) keys off.** **v3, 2026-11-16: the brand/off-site layer in that sentence is RETIRED. Read it as: hold the moat, win the hub with on-site depth, and ship a new post only where the §5 demand test passes.**

## 5. Content Standards

- **Depth doctrine (v2): consolidate before creating.** "Query Deserves a Page" (Gübür, Jul 2026): merging overlapping pages lifts rankings where adding pages does not; topical authority is hyperfocus, not page count (Indig). Before any new URL, ask whether an existing page should own the intent. A standing `prune-merge` queue type audits the post set for overlap clusters and consolidates them (301s via the p5 pattern).
- **Cadence and the demand test (v3, 2026-11-16; replaces the v2 cadence bullet).** The daily run produces work, not necessarily a URL. New posts ship at most 2 per week, and only when a new-post item is the first eligible item in the queue (§6 rule 1). A new post ships only if all three of these hold:
  - **(a) Evidence of demand.** People search the phrase, shown by our own Search Console data, by Google autocomplete (suggestqueries) or by a recurring forum question (Reddit counts). A topic is scored on product fit as well as demand.
  - **(b) No page of ours already ranks for it.** Check blogPosts.json, the root pages and pillars.json, and the weekly refresh's data. The lesson is `/blog/short-bible-verses-for-lock-screen`: its phrase had real demand, but the pillar `/daily-scripture-lock-screen` already ranked for it, and Google has never indexed the post.
  - **(c) Product fit.** The topic ends naturally at a lock screen, wallpaper or widget that FaithWall makes. If it doesn't, don't write it.
  - **(d) Information gain, named before drafting** (adopted 2026-11-16 from the medix SEO system). Before any drafting, the run writes one line naming what the post adds that the current top results lack, and where it comes from (for example the verses drawn as real lock screens, the verses checked against what fits under the clock, or setup steps tested on the current iOS). If it can't name one, it drops the topic and notes why. The §5 effort signals are checked again after drafting.
  - **Autocomplete is proof of demand, never a volume.** Harvest it politely: at most 15 requests per run, 1.5 seconds apart, stopping at the first error or block. Never write or invent a search volume.
  - **Day-one links.** Each new post gets in-body contextual links from at least two already-indexed pages that rank, in the same commit, within the §4 rule 4 cap of 5 and its replacement rule (§4 rule 8). The lesson: `lock-screen-verse-for-grief` was indexed 5 days after p58 added in-body links.
  - **Seasonal posts publish 3-5 weeks before the season** and are refreshed each year.
  - The other daily slots go to depth-passes, refreshes, CTR tests and consolidation. A daily stream of listicle/comparison URLs is the enforcement-profile pattern (§9) — velocity of *improvement* is safe; velocity of *new thin pages* is not.
- **Formats that win, in order:** root-level Compare-template roundups; pillar landing pages; the "N verses for X" listicle hybrid (depth, not a traffic engine); how-to spokes. New roundups ship at root level on the Compare template.
- **Decision-page furniture** on every transactional page: dated ranked-list title, bolded Quick Answer naming FaithWall in sentence one, comparison table, visible FAQ block, visible updated date.
- **Bias disclosure:** every roundup where FaithWall appears carries one visible line near the Quick Answer: FaithWall is our app — the ranking reflects that, here is our honest case. Note the 2026 limit of this play: our own roundups can be *cited* by AI engines yet excluded from the *recommendation*; disclosure wins trust with humans and Google, but the AI recommendation is won by third parties saying it (§8). Both are needed; neither substitutes for the other. (v3, 2026-11-16: §8 is retired, so we keep the disclosure and accept that the third-party half is out of our hands.)
- **Effort signals (v2, mandatory):** every new or refreshed page carries at least two things a scraper cannot fake — a measured first-party fact (download size, tested offline behavior, version-specific setting path, real app data), a dated hands-on observation, an original screenshot (when available), or a named-author judgment call with reasoning. The test: "would this be expensive for someone else to fake?" (Indig). Author identity: the full Person schema and AuthorBio stay on everything; the byline is a real person with a track record, never a house name.
- **Effort signals from a device-less run (mandatory — added 2026-08-30 after the p21 incident).** The cloud agent has no iPhone and no simulator. It must **never** write a first-person testing, timing, or device-behaviour claim: no "we tested", "in our test", "we timed", "we backed out to the home screen", no "confirmed background playback", no observed timings or battery/offline results. When a page would need device evidence, it satisfies the two-signal rule above from what it can honestly source — App Store listing facts (price, size, version, release date, publisher), publisher documentation quoted and attributed, iOS setting paths, a named-author judgment call with stated reasoning, or an original screenshot **only if one already exists in the repo** — and writes the device test that is actually needed as a dated line in `TODO-KAROL.md` for Karol, instead of inventing it. A page that cannot honestly reach two signals ships with one plus that TODO line; it does not manufacture the second. **This rule outranks the two-signal minimum.** An unverifiable first-person claim is a hard merge-gate FAIL, and a failed branch blocks every subsequent autopilot run — the p21 branch cost 08-23 → 08-30: eight days, zero output.
- **Length:** standard posts 800–1,100 words. Comparison-intent spokes to ~1,400 when coverage requires. Money pages and the hub are exempt upward — their `depth-pass` target is genuine comprehensiveness (2,500+ words with tables, FAQ, per-app detail), because depth correlates directly with AI citation rates. Never pad: depth = more sub-questions fully answered.
- **Freshness:** money pages get a materially-true visible update monthly; other roundups quarterly; dated titles roll forward each January. Freshness is now also the cheapest AI-citation lever (Allsopp: cited lists skew heavily recently-updated).
- **AEO (passage-level, per King):** one quotable self-contained positioning sentence in the first two paragraphs; open each H2 with a direct answer in the first 40–60 words; keep key passages self-contained at ~130–170 words; name entities (apps, features, iOS versions) explicitly — retrieval is passage-level and rank-independent, so every section is its own citation candidate. ## 11. Conversion and measurement layer (v3.1, 2026-11-16)

Built in b73c084 (journal 39, evening update). Without backlinks, Search sends what it sends. This layer makes those visits count and makes the result measurable. Code enforces each rule where it can.

**One facts file.** `src/data/facts.json` holds the product facts: store name, app id, provider token, minimum iOS (15.0; the widget needs iOS 16), the install and subscription lines, and the rating. Components, the home schema and the lint read it, and no product fact is typed into a component. `scripts/check-facts.mjs` runs weekly in the refresh. It compares the file with Apple's lookup and may rewrite `rating.*` only. The site shows a rating only when Apple's live value is at least 4.5 with at least 20 ratings. The script exits 2 when the store title or the minimum iOS changes, because copy depends on them, and exits 1 when the lookup has no rating.

**Gates.** `npm run lint:facts` (`scripts/lint-facts.mjs`) runs on the diff while writing and on `dist/` after a build. It fails on:
- banned claims: a free app, prices or trials, iOS 16 as the minimum, the app picking verses for you, a rotating wallpaper, a widget that rotates your own set, unverified features (packs, offline, ad-free and the like), and "no account";
- an App Store link without `pt` and a listed `ct`;
- more than one MobileApplication node, a node outside `/`, or any aggregateRating;
- NUL bytes, a 404 page without noindex, a sitemap URL without a built file, and a mid card on a held page.

FAIL blocks a merge. A list item that starts with another app's bold name and never names FaithWall is exempt from the bare-phrase rules.

**Campaign tokens.** A fixed list of 12, never per page: `web-home-hero`, `web-site`, `web-blog-body`, `web-blog-mid`, `web-blog-end`, `web-blog-sticky`, `web-pillar`, `web-cmp`, `web-maker`, `web-random`, `web-smart-banner`, `web-handoff`. `ct` is at most 30 characters, Apple's limit, and `appStoreUrl()` throws on a longer token at build time. Adding a token is a strategy change: add it here and in `src/lib/appStore.ts` together.

**Placement.**
- Mid card: at most one per treated post. It goes before the first H2, from the third one on, that is at least 3 sections after an illustration, not right after an image, more than 3 sections from any CTA block, and below at least 30% of the article's text. If no H2 qualifies, there is no mid card.
- End card: every treated post, built from the post's own CTA copy plus the facts lines.
- The random-verse tool shows a card after the first draw.
- Cards carry `data-nosnippet` (never article text) and contain no headings. On Android they show the badge plus "FaithWall is made for iPhone." On desktop they add a QR code and Copy link (`web-handoff`).
- Phones get a sticky "Get the app" bar after one screen of scrolling. It hides over cards, held CTAs and the footer, and never shows on Android.

Membership never changes before p72. Until then, every other change to a treated or held page gets a row in the private `annotations.csv`, so the read-out can drop that page. The read-out compares treated and held pages in Search Console before and after 11-16, privately. The conversion read is Karol's monthly App Store Connect campaigns read.

**After every deploy that changes pages (the pinging checklist).**
1. Wait until the new build is live. Check that the changed URLs answer 200 and an unknown URL answers 404 with noindex.
2. Run `node scripts/submit-indexnow.mjs --changed <base>`; a site-wide file prints "run --all". IndexNow reaches Bing (which feeds ChatGPT search and Copilot), Yandex, Seznam, Naver and Yep.
